I picked up for the first time The Pragmatic Programmer years ago,
while I was still a tester at Electronic Arts.  I don't remember
how I heard about the book.

Recently I gave it another go and picked up the 20th Anniversary
Edition, since I had the time and I wasn't pleased with the first
pass,  Hoping the intervening experiences would shed some light on
what I missed the first time.

Suffice to say, this book changed me and the way I am as a professional.
It was surprising to see parts of the book have marked me so deeply
after the first reading.  I am mainly talking about the first three
chapters.

First, the authors lay out the pragmatic philosophy.  How can I
summaries it?  The developer has to take responsibility for the code
he writes, he has to affect positive change on the projects he works
on.  A pragmatic programmer is solely responsible for his education
("knowledge portfolio").  He must be able to clearly communicate his
ideas base on the context.

The section about entropy explain it does not affect only physical
systems, but it is a reality in software projects.  Here, the concept
of broken windows spoke to me on a personal level and it stuck with
me, so much, I though the idea was de-emphasized in later editions.
The advice here is: "Don't Live with Broken Windows".

Another rule this chapter remind me of is "The Boy Scout rule" from
"Clean Code".  The two books having some overlay.

If there is a book I would recommend to a young, aspiring programmer,
this is it; I would place it above most other general programming
books for it's accessibility and conciseness.
